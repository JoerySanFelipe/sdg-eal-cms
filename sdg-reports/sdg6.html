Closed-Loop Water Architecture

Water is a critical global resource, and Urdaneta City University is committed to drastically reducing its municipal aquifer consumption. By retrofitting our facilities with sensor-driven, low-flow fixtures and deploying an extensive rainwater harvesting matrix across major campus buildings, we have successfully created a localized, closed-loop water system.

Our centralized graywater treatment facility ensures that runoff from sanitation and cafeteria use is rigorously filtered and repurposed exclusively for campus landscaping and non-potable flushing. This engineering drastically suppresses our ecological footprint and guarantees long-term sustainability.

Community Sanitation & WASH Protocols

Beyond our architectural achievements, UCU actively propagates Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) standards throughout surrounding municipalities. Our engineering and community extension departments frequently deploy to rural barangays to assess ground-water toxicity and install low-cost, high-efficiency filtration systems.

The university also maintains stringent internal sanitary protocols. Drinking stations across campus are subjected to rigorous microbiological testing every quarter, ensuring that 100% of our student population has unimpeded access to safe, potable hydration.
